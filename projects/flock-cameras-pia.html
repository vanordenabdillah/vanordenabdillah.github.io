Privacy Impact Assessment: Flock Safety Cameras

Paper / write-up

A privacy impact assessment of Flock Safety's automated license plate reader (ALPR) camera network, examining how the system collects, retains, and shares data.

Overview

This paper evaluates the privacy implications of Flock Safety's camera network, which uses automated license plate recognition to log vehicle movements across a network of fixed and municipally-deployed cameras. The assessment walks through what data the system collects, how long it's retained, who it's shared with (including cross-jurisdiction sharing between police departments), and what oversight or audit mechanisms exist.

The analysis is framed around a standard privacy impact assessment structure - describing the system, identifying the personal data involved, assessing necessity and proportionality, and surfacing risks such as function creep, chilling effects on movement and association, and the lack of meaningful consent for people captured by the cameras.
